<div class="card" id="core-idea">
  <h2>Core Idea</h2>
  <p>Your body can organize around movement. This mode prepares you to act, respond, push, and engage. It sharpens focus and directs energy outward. <strong>Sympathetic Lead Mode</strong> is when the system is oriented toward doing. This is not a problem — it is necessary.</p>
</div>

<div class="card" id="the-process">
  <h2>What It Feels Like</h2>
  <ol>
    <li>Faster breathing and increased alertness.</li>
    <li>Tighter focus and quicker reactions.</li>
    <li>More urgency, stronger physical readiness.</li>
    <li>The system preparing for output.</li>
  </ol>
</div>

<div class="card" id="why-it-matters">
  <h2>When It Is Useful</h2>
  <p>This mode is helpful when you need to work with intensity, respond quickly, perform, protect yourself, or engage with something demanding. It lets you move fast and act decisively.</p>
</div>

<div class="card" id="failure-mode">
  <h2>Failure Mode</h2>
  <strong>Staying activated after the moment has passed</strong>
  <p>The issue is not activation — it is staying there. That looks like: wired but tired, looping thoughts, constant scanning, tension that does not release. The system is still preparing for something that is no longer happening.</p>
</div>

<div class="card" id="practice">
  <h2>Practice</h2>
  <p>When the moment passes, help the system shift:</p>
  <ul>
    <li>reduce input</li>
    <li>slow pace</li>
    <li>soften focus</li>
    <li>allow the body to settle</li>
  </ul>
  <p>You are not forcing calm. You are allowing the system to stop pushing. The skill is knowing when to stop acting.</p>
</div>
