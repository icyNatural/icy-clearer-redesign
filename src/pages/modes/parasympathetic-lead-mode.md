<div class="card" id="core-idea">
  <h2>Core Idea</h2>
  <p>Your body has different operating modes. One mode helps you push, react, and act quickly. Another helps you recover, breathe deeper, digest, and settle. <strong>Parasympathetic Lead Mode</strong> is when the body stops organizing around threat and starts organizing around rhythm again. It is not weakness. It is not passivity. It is the system returning to a state where recovery can happen.</p>
</div>

<div class="card" id="the-process">
  <h2>The Process</h2>
  <ol>
    <li><strong>Signal drops.</strong> The system stops scanning so hard.</li>
    <li><strong>Breath lengthens.</strong> Exhales get longer, the diaphragm engages.</li>
    <li><strong>Body softens.</strong> Muscle tone releases, thought slows.</li>
    <li><strong>Repair resumes.</strong> Digestion, integration, and recovery come back online.</li>
  </ol>
</div>

<div class="card" id="why-it-matters">
  <h2>Why It Matters</h2>
  <p>The issue is not sympathetic activation — that is useful when the moment calls for it. The issue is staying activated when it is no longer needed. Without a working return path, the body keeps preparing for something that is no longer happening.</p>
</div>

<div class="card" id="failure-mode">
  <h2>Failure Mode</h2>
  <strong>Forcing calm instead of allowing rhythm</strong>
  <p>Trying to think your way into calm keeps the analytic system online. Lyrics, problem-solving, or self-monitoring all pull the body back toward alertness. The goal is not to <em>perform</em> calm — it is to give the system a rhythm it can trust.</p>
</div>

<div class="card" id="practice">
  <h2>Practice</h2>
  <p>Put on steady, gentle instrumental music. Do not try to control the breath. Just notice:</p>
  <ul>
    <li>where the breath moves</li>
    <li>whether the exhale lengthens</li>
    <li>whether the belly or diaphragm engages</li>
    <li>whether the body softens</li>
    <li>whether thought stops leading</li>
  </ul>
  <p>Let rhythm take over gradually.</p>
</div>
